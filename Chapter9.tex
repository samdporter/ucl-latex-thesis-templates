\subsection{Relative Nuclear Variation}
\label{subsec:future_rnv}

The modality weights used by \wdtnv{} compensate for the markedly different global scales of the PET and SPECT images. However, a single global normalisation does not account for the large spatial variation in activity within either image. An extension inspired by the \acrfull{rdp} \cite{nuytsConcavePriorPenalizing2002} could introduce this local intensity dependence while retaining the edge-alignment mechanism of the nuclear norm. We refer to the proposed construction as \emph{relative nuclear variation} (\acrfull{rnv}).

Let $\mathcal{E}=\{e_\ell\}_{\ell=1}^{d}$ be the same stencil used by the finite-difference operator $D$ in Chapter~\ref{chapter:paper}. Alongside $D$, define a companion endpoint-sum operator $Z$ by
\begin{equation}
    \bigl((Z z_m)_n\bigr)^{(\ell)}
    :=
    \frac{z_{m,n+e_\ell}+z_{m,n}}{\|e_\ell\|_\Delta},
    \qquad \ell=1,\ldots,d.
    \label{eq:rnv_sum_operator}
\end{equation}
Thus, $Z$ uses the same neighbourhood, boundary treatment and physical distance scaling as $D$, differing only in replacing subtraction by addition. Under the Neumann extension used in Chapter~\ref{chapter:paper}, an out-of-domain neighbour is replaced by the boundary voxel itself, so the corresponding endpoint sum is $2z_{m,n}$. The same fixed PET/SPECT weights that scale the columns of the weighted directional Jacobian should also scale their contributions to the local sum.
Define
\begin{equation}
    s_n(\bm{z})
    :=
    \sum_{m=1}^{M}\rho_{m,n}
    \sum_{\ell=1}^{d}
    \bigl((Z z_m)_n\bigr)^{(\ell)},
    \label{eq:rnv_local_sum}
\end{equation}
The full directional sum is retained so that its stencil scaling matches that of the locally quadratic numerator. In particular, duplicating every stencil direction $q$ times scales $r_n^2$ and $s_n$ by the same factor $q$, leaving $r_n^2/s_n$ unchanged. The weights $\rho_{m,n}$ are fixed with respect to $\bm{z}$ but may be voxel-dependent; the application in Chapter~\ref{chapter:paper} would use the spatially uniform weights $\rho_m$. Thus, $s_n$ is the direct sum counterpart of the weighted finite-difference bank: it retains the PET/SPECT modality scaling but does not apply the CT-derived directional operators $\Pi_{m,n}$. Anatomical directionality continues to enter the numerator through the weighted directional Jacobian.

Let
\begin{equation}
    r_n(\bm{z})
    :=
    \left\|\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_n(\bm{z})\right\|_*
    =
    \sum_{\ell=1}^{\min(d,M)}
    \sigma_\ell\!\left(\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_n(\bm{z})\right)
    \label{eq:rnv_nuclear_magnitude}
\end{equation}
be the nuclear magnitude of the same weighted directional Jacobian used by \wdtnv, including the modality weights $\rho_{m,n}$ and CT-derived operators $\Pi_{m,n}$. An \gls{rnv} prior could then be defined as
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{RNV}}(\bm{z})
    :=
    V_{\mathrm{vox}}
    \sum_{n=1}^{N}
    \frac{r_n(\bm{z})^2}
    {s_n(\bm{z})+\gamma_{\mathrm{RNV}}r_n(\bm{z})
        +\varepsilon_{\mathrm{RNV}}},
    \qquad
    \gamma_{\mathrm{RNV}}>0,\quad
    \varepsilon_{\mathrm{RNV}}>0.
    \label{eq:rnv_prior}
\end{equation}
This is inspired by the scalar RDP construction, but aggregates the multimodality directional information into a single nuclear magnitude rather than summing pairwise RDP terms. Because $D$ and $Z$ use the same physical distance scaling and the same modality weights, $r_n$ and $s_n$ have the same units; $\gamma_{\mathrm{RNV}}$ is dimensionless and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{RNV}}$ has the units of $r_n$. For $r_n\ll(s_n+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{RNV}})/\gamma_{\mathrm{RNV}}$, the local density would behave approximately as $r_n^2/(s_n+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{RNV}})$ and therefore provide locally intensity-scaled quadratic regularisation of the nuclear gradient. For $r_n\gg(s_n+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{RNV}})/\gamma_{\mathrm{RNV}}$, it approaches $r_n/\gamma_{\mathrm{RNV}}$ and hence recovers TNV-like linear edge penalisation.

The proposed non-negative penalty would be convex under the definitions above. To see this, consider
\begin{equation}
    h(r,s)
    :=
    \frac{r^2}{s+\gamma_{\mathrm{RNV}}r+
        \varepsilon_{\mathrm{RNV}}},
    \qquad r\geq0,\quad s\geq0.
\end{equation}
Writing $q=s+\gamma_{\mathrm{RNV}}r+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{RNV}}$, its Hessian with respect to $(r,s)$ is
\begin{equation}
    \nabla^2 h(r,s)
    =
    \frac{2}{q^3}
    \begin{bmatrix}
        s+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{RNV}}\\
        -r
    \end{bmatrix}
    \begin{bmatrix}
        s+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{RNV}} & -r
    \end{bmatrix}
    \succeq 0.
    \label{eq:rnv_hessian}
\end{equation}
Moreover, $h$ is non-decreasing in $r$. The mapping $\bm{z}\mapsto r_n(\bm{z})$ is convex because the nuclear norm is convex and $\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_n$ is linear in $\bm{z}$, while $\bm{z}\mapsto s_n(\bm{z})$ is non-negative and affine when the weights in Equation~\eqref{eq:rnv_local_sum} are fixed. Their composition in Equation~\eqref{eq:rnv_prior} is therefore convex on the non-negative image domain.

Further work is required to determine whether the local adaptation improves the bias--noise trade-off in regions with heterogeneous uptake without allowing the activity scale of one modality to inappropriately weaken regularisation of the other. This should include derivation of the \gls{rnv} gradient and prior-aware curvature models, and controlled simulations containing shared and modality-unique edges. The spatially adaptive \gls{rnv} formulation should also be compared with a simpler baseline in which $s_n$ is estimated from initial PET and SPECT reconstructions and then frozen. Finally, the unsmoothed nuclear norm in Equation~\eqref{eq:rnv_nuclear_magnitude} makes the relationship to the classical RDP explicit but is not differentiable at all rank-deficient Jacobians. A differentiable convex approximation suitable for the optimisation framework of Chapter~\ref{chapter:optimisation} would therefore need to be derived. Directly substituting the Charbonnier-smoothed spectral magnitude used by \wdtnv{} would alter the limiting behaviour at very small gradients and should be evaluated rather than assumed equivalent.
