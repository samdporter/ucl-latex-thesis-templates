\chapter{Discussion, Future Work \& Conclusion}
\label{discussion}

\section{Discussion}

This thesis investigated joint post-\gls{sirt} \gls{y90} \gls{pet}/\gls{spect} reconstruction with directional guidance from an attenuation-correction \gls{ct}. The main finding was that functional coupling and anatomical directional guidance worked best together in the evaluated studies. A practical bremsstrahlung SPECT model and prior-aware optimisation supported this reconstruction, while CT quality affected the resulting images.

Anatomical guidance and functional coupling provided complementary information. In the bootstrapped \gls{nema} study of Chapter~\ref{chapter:paper}, \dtv{} (directional guidance without coupling) and \wtnv{} (coupling without directional guidance) produced broadly similar PET appearances and similar recovery, and neither recovered the smallest spheres. Combining both mechanisms in \wdtnv{} gave significantly higher recovery than either ablation for all sphere sizes $\leq\SI{22}{\milli\metre}$. This is consistent with the intended mechanism of \wdtnv: the higher-count SPECT image provides functional support for the location of activity transitions, while the $\mu$-derived direction field reduces penalisation of gradients aligned with anatomical boundaries. Neither source of side information was sufficient on its own to recover the smallest spheres in this experiment.

The deconvolution study of Chapter~\ref{chapter:deconvolution_pvc} showed the advantages of edge information in a simpler setting and exposed a separate problem for quantification. For \gls{krl} and \gls{hkrl}, the stopping iteration becomes part of the regularisation. The sphere study therefore used ground-truth \gls{nrmse} to choose the stopping point, while the brain example relied on visual assessment. Ground truth is unavailable clinically, and visual assessment does not establish quantitative accuracy. The resulting estimate depends on the iteration and, because the iterates retain information from their starting point, on the initialising image. \gls{rl}, \gls{krl} and \gls{hkrl} all changed when the initialisation changed; with the blurred \gls{pet} initialisation, the best unregularised \gls{rl} result occurred at iteration zero. \gls{rldtv}, in contrast, reached the same optima under the two tested initialisations and achieved the lowest \gls{nrmse}, the highest aggregate \gls{rc} and the lowest background \gls{cov}.

The same issue appears in the full reconstruction. \gls{shkem} in Chapter~\ref{chapter:paper} is evaluated at a selected subiteration chosen to approximately match the vendor reconstruction's background \gls{cov}. \wdtnv{}, by contrast, is iterated towards the minimiser of a fixed objective, with the operating point set by the regularisation weights. Both approaches require a choice, but the penalised formulation separates regularisation from the stopping rule. Whether the selected weights transfer to other acquisition protocols remains to be tested.

CT was used for direction rather than as a third intensity channel. Chapter~\ref{chapter:wdtnv} keeps those two roles separate: $\Pi_{m,n}$ supplies anatomical direction and $B_n$ handles modality scale. The voxel-wise Jacobian therefore has two modality columns, so the spectral calculation reduces to a $2\times2$ inverse square root. Modality weights balance the two emission channels without requiring an additional CT intensity weight.

Chapter~\ref{chapterlabel3} combined established scatter-estimation methods with a learned initialisation, under-relaxed reconstruct--simulate refinement and a calibrated effective range kernel to prepare the SPECT model used in the joint study. Under-relaxation kept the non-primary estimates bounded over the tested refinement budget, and the range kernel reduced discrepancies in projected response widths. These steps addressed some of the bremsstrahlung blur and scatter-estimation difficulties, but did not remove them. A joint prior can also share systematic modelling error, so residual SPECT errors may bias the PET reconstruction.

This suggests a possible role for bremsstrahlung SPECT beyond producing a standalone activity image: its higher counts may be useful as functional guidance for low-count PET, with CT supplying anatomical direction. SPECT may be more useful in this supporting role than as a standalone quantitative reconstruction, given its residual blur and uncertainty in scatter estimation. This is a hypothesis, not a comparison established by the thesis; SPECT quantification was not evaluated, and coupling does not prevent modelling errors from being transferred.

In Chapter~\ref{chapter:optimisation}, data-only \gls{bsrem} scaling diverged in the count-limited PET channel under the tested step-size schedule, while both prior-aware models remained bounded and continued to reduce regional error. SPECT was more robust, and data-only scaling remained competitive there. Thus the same prior produced different effective conditioning in the two channels. Retaining the within-voxel PET/SPECT curvature gave only modest gains over the diagonal model, suggesting that inclusion of the prior mattered more than the specific form of that inclusion. The folded schedule reduced error over the tested interval, but differing computational costs prevent a claim of improved time efficiency.

In the clinical cohort, \wdtnv{} yielded significantly higher lesion \gls{tbr} than \dtv, \wtnv{} and \gls{shkem} across 45 lesions in nine patients, using operating points selected to approximately match the training patient's vendor background \gls{cov}. The margin over \gls{shkem}, which also uses CT and SPECT guidance, was the smallest. In the phantom, \gls{shkem} had similar recovery to \wdtnv{} for spheres of \SI{22}{\milli\metre} and above; significant differences were confined to \SI{13}{\milli\metre} and \SI{10}{\milli\metre}. There was a reproducibility trade-off: at the selected operating points, the more strongly smoothing \dtv{} and \wtnv{} baselines had lower voxel-wise \gls{cov} than \wdtnv{}.

Lesion-level responses varied. In the examined examples, lesions with clear anatomical correlates, such as adjacent calcification or a liver capsule boundary, benefited more; lesions without a corresponding structure in the $\mu$-map improved less. This pattern is consistent with CTAC guidance favouring edge directions where anatomical boundaries are present. Since the directional weighting field $\|\xi\|$ is available before reconstruction, its relationship to lesion-level benefit could be investigated.

The motivation for joint reconstruction is the complementary information in the modalities. PET- and SPECT-based mean absorbed doses are highly correlated across liver \glspl{voi} \cite{kappadathQuantitativeEvaluation90YPET2024}, but the same comparison reports a systematic offset: PET gives, on average, a $14\%$ higher tumour mean dose and a lower perfused-normal-liver mean dose than SPECT. That lesion-level difference is consistent with the different resolution and partial-volume behaviour of the two modalities. Dosimetry-guided prescription \cite{salemClinicalDosimetricConsiderations2019} and wider implementation of dosimetry \cite{gearEANMEnablingGuide2023} motivate more reliable lesion-level activity estimates. Higher scanner sensitivity may reduce count limitation for PET at some centres, but does not address the modelling difficulties specific to bremsstrahlung SPECT.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{subsec:discussion_limitations}

A principal limitation is the sensitivity of the directional field to CTAC quality. Because $\xi$ is derived from $D\mu$, coherent CT artefacts such as streaking resulting from a scan with the patient arms down are reproduced in $\|\xi\|$ and can enter the emission reconstructions, as observed in the training patient of Chapter~\ref{chapter:paper}. The stabiliser $\eta$ limits amplification of small gradients but cannot reliably suppress structured, high-contrast artefacts. Where a low-dose CTAC is used for guidance, acquisition with arms raised where feasible is preferable. The sensitivity of \wdtnv{} to residual PET/SPECT/CT registration error was not characterised. A prior that rewards aligned edges may introduce bias when those edges are misregistered, so this requires explicit study.

The evaluation protocol also carries limitations that follow from the decision to preserve comparability with \gls{shkem}. Regularisation weights were selected once on a single training patient and then fixed, which avoids repeated tuning on the evaluation cohort but does not establish the robustness of those weights across acquisition protocols and lesion presentations. Quantitative endpoints were restricted to PET, so the jointly reconstructed SPECT images, although visually less noisy while retaining lesion conspicuity, were assessed qualitatively only. Recovery, \gls{tbr} and \gls{cov} describe image quality; they do not establish absorbed-dose accuracy or clinical benefit, and no dose-level or outcome-level analysis was performed. Finally, the clinical cohort is single-centre and retrospective, and the thesis compares against variational and kernel-based methods but not against learned reconstruction or learned correction approaches \cite{hashimotoPETImageReconstruction2022,jia90YSPECTScatter2023}.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future_work}

\subsection{Relative Nuclear Variation}
\label{subsec:future_rnv}

The modality weights used by \wdtnv{} compensate for the markedly different global scales of the PET and SPECT images, but do not adapt to spatial variation in activity within either image. One possible extension, inspired by the \acrfull{rdp} \cite{nuytsConcavePriorPenalizing2002}, would scale the coupled edge magnitude by a local activity measure. Let $r_n(\bm{z})=\|\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_n(\bm{z})\|_*$ denote the nuclear magnitude of the weighted directional Jacobian used by \wdtnv{}, and let $s_n(\bm{z})\geq0$ denote a weighted local activity sum over the same finite-difference neighbourhood. A proposed \acrfull{rnv} penalty is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{RNV}}(\bm{z})
    :=
    V_{\mathrm{vox}}
    \sum_{n=1}^{N}
    \frac{r_n(\bm{z})^2}
    {s_n(\bm{z})+\gamma_{\mathrm{RNV}}r_n(\bm{z})
        +\varepsilon_{\mathrm{RNV}}},
    \qquad
    \gamma_{\mathrm{RNV}}>0,\quad
    \varepsilon_{\mathrm{RNV}}>0.
    \label{eq:rnv_prior}
\end{equation}
The construction is ``relative'' because the penalty on a coupled edge is moderated by the local activity scale. For fixed non-negative modality weights on the non-negative image domain, the scalar density $r^2/(s+\gamma_{\mathrm{RNV}}r+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{RNV}})$ is jointly convex in $(r,s)$ and non-decreasing in $r$; since $r_n$ is convex and $s_n$ is affine, the proposed penalty is convex under these assumptions. This construction was not implemented or evaluated in this thesis, and whether such local scaling improves the bias--noise trade-off without weakening cross-modality regularisation inappropriately remains to be established.

\subsection{Robustness and Clinical Translation}
\label{subsec:future_translation}

The first methodological priority is to make the directional field less sensitive to CTAC quality. Estimating $\xi$ from a denoised, artefact-corrected or segmentation-derived attenuation map could reduce transfer of coherent streak artefacts while retaining useful anatomical directions. Registration error is a second unresolved issue. Controlled rigid and deformable perturbations of the SPECT-to-PET warp would show how much lesion-level bias the prior introduces; if that effect is material, joint reconstruction and registration could be investigated. The optimisation study raises a third question: PET and SPECT responded differently to the same preconditioner and step-size schedule, so modality-dependent preconditioner scaling or step sizes are worth testing.

The framework could also be tested beyond the favourable case in which PET and SPECT image the same underlying \gls{y90} microsphere distribution. Shared anatomy with non-identical functional distributions, including \gls{maa} planning versus post-treatment imaging, multi-nuclide studies, or multiple timepoints, may require weaker or spatially adaptive coupling so that modality-specific or evolving uptake is not suppressed. Evaluation should also extend from image-quality endpoints to absorbed dose. Voxel-level dose kernels, dose--volume measures, and a multi-centre cohort with response data would test whether the lesion-level improvements translate into more reliable dosimetry.

Further comparisons could clarify the role of SPECT and its modelling limitations. The effective range kernel of Chapter~\ref{chapterlabel3} is isotropic and water-equivalent; comparison with an anisotropic, material-dependent bremsstrahlung model such as that of Lim \textit{et al.} \cite{lim_y-90_2018} could help assess the effect of that approximation. Comparing standalone SPECT and SPECT-guided PET using activity and dose endpoints would test the proposed supporting role of SPECT. Comparisons with learned reconstruction or learned correction methods \cite{hashimotoPETImageReconstruction2022,jia90YSPECTScatter2023} would also place the framework alongside approaches not evaluated here.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:thesis_conclusion}

This thesis developed and evaluated joint post-\gls{sirt} \gls{y90} PET/SPECT reconstruction with CT-derived directional guidance. Four contributions support this framework.

First, Chapter~\ref{chapterlabel3} combined established methods with practical refinements to the bremsstrahlung SPECT model. A learned projection estimate from a network trained on a different acquisition protocol initialised SIMIND estimation of non-primary counts; under-relaxed updates kept those estimates bounded over the refinement budget where full replacement did not; and a calibrated effective Gaussian range kernel reduced projected response-width discrepancies with GATE and SIMIND across three energy windows. The model components were then fixed for joint reconstruction. These changes addressed selected sources of error without resolving all SPECT modelling limitations.

Second, Chapter~\ref{chapter:wdtnv} introduced \wdtnv, a smoothed, weighted and directional form of \gls{tnv}. Charbonnier smoothing gives a differentiable penalty whose Jacobian-space gradient can be evaluated through a $2\times2$ Gram matrix for two emission modalities, avoiding a per-voxel \gls{svd}. The formulation separates anatomical direction from modality scale, so that CT steers the penalty without entering as a coupled intensity channel. This choice was supported by the controlled deconvolution study of Chapter~\ref{chapter:deconvolution_pvc}, where an explicit directional penalty outperformed the tested kernelised methods and reached the same optima under two initialisations.

Third, Chapter~\ref{chapter:optimisation} compared subset organisation, prior-application schedules and prior-aware preconditioning in controlled single-bed phantom experiments. With expected data exposure and relaxation history matched, the prior-application schedule mattered more than the subset organisation over the tested interval; differing computational costs mean that this does not establish a time-efficiency advantage. Prior-aware preconditioning kept the count-limited PET updates bounded where data-only scaling diverged under the tested step-size schedule. Retaining the within-voxel PET/SPECT curvature gave only modest gains over a diagonal model.

Fourth, Chapter~\ref{chapter:paper} extended the framework to joint multi-bed PET reconstruction, so that the edge-preserving prior acts across the bed overlap, and evaluated it on \gls{y90} phantom and patient data. On a bootstrapped \gls{nema} phantom, \wdtnv{} significantly improved recovery of spheres of \SI{22}{\milli\metre} and below relative to \dtv{} and \wtnv, and improved on \gls{shkem} at the two smallest sphere sizes. In a cohort of 45 lesions across nine post-\gls{sirt} patients, \wdtnv{} gave significantly higher lesion \gls{tbr} than all three comparators. These comparisons used fixed operating points selected to approximately match the training patient's vendor background \gls{cov}. The supporting simulation and reconstruction software is documented in Appendix~\ref{app:software}.

The main finding is that PET/SPECT coupling and CT-derived directional guidance worked best together in the evaluated studies. CT supplied useful anatomical directions, but its quality also mattered: artefacts in the guidance could enter the emission images. Improvements to the bremsstrahlung SPECT model enabled its use in this framework, while residual blur and scatter-estimation uncertainty motivate the hypothesis that SPECT may be more useful as functional guidance for PET than as a standalone quantitative image. That comparison remains untested. The studies demonstrated gains in PET recovery and lesion contrast, but clinical evaluation was single-centre. Improved absorbed-dose accuracy and robustness to CT artefacts and residual registration error remain to be established.
