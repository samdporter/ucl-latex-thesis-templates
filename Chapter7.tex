\chapter{Optimisation for Synergistic Reconstruction}
\label{chapter:optimisation}

Chapter~\ref{chapter:wdtnv} defined the smoothed weighted directional total nuclear variation prior. Applying it in synergistic reconstruction raises three linked questions: how to reconstruct overlapping \gls{pet} beds jointly, how to organise modality subsets and schedule the prior, and how to include prior curvature in the preconditioner. Over the common 20-pass protocol, the folded prior schedule was the only tested schedule that reduced \gls{nrmse}, prior-aware scaling kept count-limited \gls{pet} bounded, and no prior-curvature model was a universal winner.

\section{Multi-Bed \gls{pet} Acquisition}
\label{sec:multibed}

Whole-liver \gls{pet} commonly requires multiple partially overlapping bed positions because a single axial field of view does not cover the organ. Structures of interest can therefore extend into the low-sensitivity edge of an individual bed, while two beds contribute measurements of the shared anatomy in their overlap. A coupled edge-preserving prior should act on one activity field spanning the acquisition, rather than on images divided by scanner bed boundaries.

\subsection{Why Not Stitch Independent Reconstructions?}
\label{subsec:stitching_failure}

A common practical alternative is to reconstruct each \gls{pet} bed independently and fuse the images using sensitivity-weighted stitching in the overlap. With an edge-preserving prior, however, each reconstruction makes its own noise-suppression and edge decisions before fusion. The qualitative example in Figure~\ref{fig:optim_multibed_qualitative} shows overlap-region noise and discontinuities after separate reconstruction and fusion. It is included to motivate the joint formulation; no quantitative comparison was performed \cite{porterSimultaneousMultiBedMAP2025}.

\subsection{Joint Multi-Bed Formulation}
\label{subsec:multibed_notation}
\label{subsec:joint_multibed}

Let $x$ be the \gls{pet} activity image on the combined reconstruction grid. For bed position $\mathrm{bed}=1,\ldots,N_{\mathrm{bed}}$, let $R_{\mathrm{bed}}$ restrict the combined image to the axial support reconstructed by that bed. The expected \gls{pet} data for bed position $\mathrm{bed}$ are
\begin{equation}
    \bar y_{\mathrm{bed}}(x)
    =
    A_{\mathrm{bed}} R_{\mathrm{bed}} x + b_{\mathrm{bed}},
    \label{eq:multibed_forward_model}
\end{equation}
where $A_{\mathrm{bed}}$ is the bed-specific \gls{pet} system matrix and $b_{\mathrm{bed}}$ collects additive corrections. The joint \gls{pet} data term is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{PET}}^{\mathrm{multi}}(x)
    =
    \sum_{\mathrm{bed}=1}^{N_{\mathrm{bed}}}
    \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{bed}}
    \left(
        y_{\mathrm{bed}} \mid A_{\mathrm{bed}} R_{\mathrm{bed}} x + b_{\mathrm{bed}}
    \right),
    \label{eq:multibed_data_term}
\end{equation}
with gradient
\begin{equation}
    \nabla \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{PET}}^{\mathrm{multi}}(x)
    =
    \sum_{\mathrm{bed}=1}^{N_{\mathrm{bed}}}
    R_{\mathrm{bed}}^\top
    A_{\mathrm{bed}}^\top
    \nabla_{\bar y_{\mathrm{bed}}}
    \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{bed}}
    \left(
        y_{\mathrm{bed}} \mid A_{\mathrm{bed}} R_{\mathrm{bed}} x + b_{\mathrm{bed}}
    \right).
    \label{eq:multibed_gradient}
\end{equation}
The corresponding \gls{pet} sensitivity image on the combined grid is
\begin{equation}
    s_{\mathrm{PET}}^{\mathrm{multi}}
    =
    \sum_{\mathrm{bed}=1}^{N_{\mathrm{bed}}}
    R_{\mathrm{bed}}^\top A_{\mathrm{bed}}^\top \bm 1.
    \label{eq:multibed_sensitivity}
\end{equation}
This sensitivity is used in the \gls{em}/\gls{bsrem} data-curvature term in exactly the same way as the single-bed sensitivity in Equation~\eqref{eq:data_hessian_surrogate}. Each bed thus contributes its own data term and sensitivity to a single combined image, while the image-domain prior is evaluated over the complete field of view. The formulation is needed for the dual-bed application in Chapter~\ref{chapter:paper}; the controlled subset and preconditioner studies below instead use a one-bed phantom to isolate optimisation effects. The preceding multi-bed example is qualitative and does not enter those comparisons.

\section{Stochastic \gls{map} Optimisation}
\label{sec:optimisation_svrg}

Let $\bm z=(z_1,z_2)$ denote the \gls{pet} and \gls{spect} images represented on a common reconstruction grid. For $N_{\mathrm d}$ sampled data functions, define $\mathcal D(\bm z)=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm d}}\mathcal D_i(\bm z)$. The objective is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{O}(\bm z)
    =
    \mathcal D(\bm z)
    +
    \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{w\text{-}dTNV}}(\bm z)
    +
    \iota_{\mathbb{R}_+}(\bm z),
    \label{eq:optimisation_objective}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{D}_i$ is a Poisson negative log-likelihood term composed with the relevant projector, image-space registration operator, and optional resolution model; $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{w\text{-}dTNV}}$ is the prior from Chapter~\ref{chapter:wdtnv}; and $\iota_{\mathbb{R}_+}$ is the indicator of the non-negative orthant, equal to zero when $\bm z\geq 0$ component-wise and $+\infty$ otherwise. The gradient estimator $g^{(k)}$ contains only the smooth terms $\mathcal D+\mathcal R$; the proximal map of the non-smooth indicator clips negative components to zero, as implemented by $\{\cdot\}_{\geq 0}$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:optimisation_projected_update}.

The stochastic experiments use \gls{svrg} \cite{johnsonAcceleratingStochasticGradient2013,twymanInvestigationStochasticVariance2022,ehrhardtFastPETReconstruction2025}. Writing $\mathcal R=\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{w\text{-}dTNV}}$, the implemented estimator keeps the complete prior outside the sampler. At anchor point $\widetilde{\bm z}$ it is
\begin{equation}
    g^{(k)} = \nabla\mathcal R(\bm z^{(k)})
    + \nabla\mathcal D(\widetilde{\bm z})
    + N_{\mathrm d}\!\left[
        \nabla\mathcal D_{i_k}(\bm z^{(k)})
        -\nabla\mathcal D_{i_k}(\widetilde{\bm z})
    \right],
    \qquad i_k\sim\operatorname{Unif}\{1,\ldots,N_{\mathrm d}\}.
    \label{eq:svrg_estimator_general}
\end{equation}
The data functions are sampled uniformly with replacement. The complete prior gradient is evaluated at every stochastic update, and the full data-gradient snapshot is refreshed once per epoch. Section~\ref{sec:optimisation_subset_selection} compares alternative prior schedules.

The image update is a projected, preconditioned gradient step,
\begin{equation}
    \bm z^{(k+1)}
    =
    \left\{
    \bm z^{(k)}
    -
    \varsigma^{(k)} \mathcal{P}^{(k)} g^{(k)}
    \right\}_{\geq 0},
    \label{eq:optimisation_projected_update}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{P}^{(k)}$ is either a diagonal preconditioner or a voxel-wise $2\times2$ block preconditioner coupling the \gls{pet} and \gls{spect} update components. The step size is iteration dependent and follows the linear decay rule
\begin{equation}
    \varsigma^{(k)}
    =
    \frac{\varsigma^{(0)}}{1+\eta_{\mathrm{relax}} k},
    \qquad
    \varsigma^{(0)}=1,
    \qquad
    \eta_{\mathrm{relax}}=0.002,
    \label{eq:optimisation_step_size}
\end{equation}
Here $\eta_{\mathrm{relax}}=0.002$ is the reference rate for the folded, modality-separated configuration, which performs 36 stochastic updates per matched data pass. The preconditioner studies use this reference configuration. The other subset configurations rescale $\eta_{\mathrm{relax}}$ to reproduce the same relaxation history on the matched-pass axis, as specified in Section~\ref{subsec:subset_selection_experiment_methods}.

\subsection{Common Evaluation Protocol}
\label{subsec:optimisation_evaluation_protocol}

The optimisation experiments used an anthropomorphic phantom scanned on the Mediso AnyScan at the National Physical Laboratory. \gls{pet} and \gls{spect} were each partitioned into 18 subsets. Each plotted comparison curve comprises five stochastic realisations and covers the first 20 complete data passes. A single 1000-epoch reconstruction of the same objective, run with more conservative optimisation settings, provided the reference image $x^\star$. Appendix~\ref{app:baseline_convergence} reports the objective and iterate diagnostics supporting its effective stationarity relative to the best observed iterate.

For each stochastic run and each saved iteration, the \acrfull{nrmse} in region $R$ is
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{NRMSE}(x_k,x^\star;R)
    =
    \frac{\sqrt{N^{-1}\sum_{j\in R}(x_{k,j}-x^\star_j)^2}}
         {\sqrt{N^{-1}\sum_{j\in R}(x^\star_j)^2}},
    \qquad N=|R|.
    \label{eq:optimisation_nrmse}
\end{equation}
The regions are evaluated separately over the whole image, the hot-sphere mask, the cold-sphere mask, and the liver compartment, shown in Figure~\ref{fig:optim_voi_overlay}. The whole-image region includes the low-activity background surrounding the phantom, and is therefore the most sensitive of the four to behaviour in voxels that carry no clinical information; the liver is the largest structure of interest and the region over which absorbed dose to normal tissue would be scored. Reporting both separates a failure confined to the background from one that reaches the anatomy. The plotted centre line is the arithmetic mean of the five per-run \gls{nrmse} values at each iteration, not the \gls{nrmse} of an averaged reconstruction; the envelope is their pointwise minimum--maximum. The long-run reference itself was generated once.

\section{Subset Selection}
\label{sec:optimisation_subset_selection}

Subset methods accelerate early iterations in emission tomography by replacing full data passes with stochastic or ordered blocks of projection data \cite{hudsonAcceleratedImageReconstruction1994}. Joint \gls{pet}/\gls{spect} reconstruction introduces two further choices: corresponding modality subsets can be paired or treated as separate stochastic functions, and the prior can be scheduled relative to those functions in different ways. The experiment crosses these two subset organisations with three prior-application schedules motivated by prior subset-selection analysis \cite{OptimisingSubsetSelection}.

\subsection{Data-Subset Organisation}
\label{subsec:subset_data_organisation}

The one-bed phantom data were divided into 18 \gls{pet} and 18 \gls{spect} subsets. Two organisations were tested.

\paragraph{Separate subsets.}
\gls{pet} and \gls{spect} subsets are treated as distinct stochastic functions. For a one-bed acquisition with $N_{\mathrm{PET}}=18$ and $N_{\mathrm{SPECT}}=18$, this gives
\begin{equation}
    \{\mathcal{D}_i\}_{i=1}^{36}
    =
    \{
    \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{PET},1},\ldots,\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{PET},18},
    \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{SPECT},1},\ldots,\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{SPECT},18}
    \}.
    \label{eq:separate_subset_functions}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Paired subsets.}
\gls{pet} and \gls{spect} subsets with matching indices are summed into one stochastic function. For one bed this gives
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{pair}}_j
    =
    \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{PET},j}
    +
    \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{SPECT},j},
    \qquad j=1,\ldots,18.
    \label{eq:paired_subset_functions}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Prior-Application Schedules}
\label{subsec:subset_prior_schedules}

Writing $N_{\mathrm d}$ for the number of data functions in the chosen organisation ($N_{\mathrm d}=36$ separate, $N_{\mathrm d}=18$ paired), define the smooth objective $\Phi=\mathcal D+\mathcal R$. Each schedule constructs a uniformly sampled component list $\{F_j\}_{j=1}^{n}$ satisfying $\sum_{j=1}^{n}F_j=\Phi$. The CIL \gls{svrg} estimator is then
\[
    g^{(k)}
    =
    \nabla\Phi(\widetilde{\bm z})
    +n\!\left[
        \nabla F_{j_k}(\bm z^{(k)})
        -\nabla F_{j_k}(\widetilde{\bm z})
    \right],
    \qquad j_k\sim\operatorname{Unif}\{1,\ldots,n\},
\]
so $\mathbb E[g^{(k)}]=\nabla\Phi(\bm z^{(k)})$ for all three lists.

\paragraph{Folded.}
Here $n=N_{\mathrm d}$ and $F_i=\mathcal D_i+\mathcal R/N_{\mathrm d}$. One expected data pass therefore comprises $N_{\mathrm d}$ updates, with the prior gradient difference entering every update. Expanding the general estimator gives Equation~\eqref{eq:svrg_estimator_general}. This is the schedule used by the preconditioner experiments later in this chapter and by the application protocol of Chapter~\ref{chapter:paper}.

\paragraph{Sampled.}
Here $n=2N_{\mathrm d}$: the list contains the $N_{\mathrm d}$ data components and $N_{\mathrm d}$ copies of $\mathcal R/N_{\mathrm d}$. Uniform sampling selects a prior-only component with probability $1/2$, so an expected data pass comprises $2N_{\mathrm d}$ updates: $N_{\mathrm d}$ data draws and $N_{\mathrm d}$ prior-only draws in expectation. A prior draw contributes twice the prior gradient difference. This schedule is labelled prior/half in the figure legend.

\paragraph{Once per epoch.}
Here $n=N_{\mathrm d}+1$: one full-prior component $\mathcal R$ is appended to the $N_{\mathrm d}$ data components. Uniform sampling gives $N_{\mathrm d}+1$ updates per expected data pass, comprising $N_{\mathrm d}$ data draws and one prior-only draw in expectation. Thus the label denotes an average frequency, not a deterministic dedicated update; a prior draw contributes $(N_{\mathrm d}+1)$ times the prior gradient difference.

\subsection{Experiment Design and Work Accounting}
\label{subsec:subset_selection_experiment_methods}

All stochastic components were sampled uniformly with replacement. One matched data pass is defined by 36 modality-specific data-subset evaluations: 18 paired data draws or 36 separate draws. For folded, sampled, and once-per-epoch scheduling, respectively, one expected data pass therefore requires $N_{\mathrm d}$, $2N_{\mathrm d}$, and $N_{\mathrm d}+1$ total stochastic updates. These are 18, 36, and 19 updates for paired organisation, and 36, 72, and 37 for separate organisation.

Let $L_q$ denote the number of stochastic updates per matched pass for configuration $q$. Since $k$ in Equation~\eqref{eq:optimisation_step_size} counts stochastic updates, the relaxation rate was normalised as
\[
    \eta_{\mathrm{relax}}^{(q)}
    =0.002\,\frac{36}{L_q}.
\]
At pass $p$, $k_q=L_qp$ and hence $\eta_{\mathrm{relax}}^{(q)}k_q=0.002\times36p$; every curve therefore has the same step size at a given matched pass. The plotted axis is matched by projection work and relaxation history, but not by total update count or computation. All configurations used the same smooth objective and the \gls{mm} local-diagonal curvature preconditioner, and were assessed with the common 20-pass protocol in Section~\ref{subsec:optimisation_evaluation_protocol}.

\subsection{Results and Interpretation}

Figure~\ref{fig:optim_subset_organisation} separates the six configurations mainly by prior schedule. The folded runs reduce mean \gls{nrmse} over the tested 20 passes; separate-folded attains the lowest mean \gls{nrmse} at pass 20 in every region for both modalities, with paired-folded close behind. The separate-folded \gls{pet} whole-image \gls{nrmse} curve has a transient jump near pass six before recovering. Neither the sampled nor once-per-epoch schedule shows a net \gls{nrmse} reduction over the same interval.

The prior correction gives one precise difference between the schedules. Let $\Delta_{\mathcal R}=\nabla\mathcal R(\bm z^{(k)})-\nabla\mathcal R(\widetilde{\bm z})$. Its contribution is $\Delta_{\mathcal R}$ at every folded update, either zero or $2\Delta_{\mathcal R}$ under sampled scheduling, and either zero or $(N_{\mathrm d}+1)\Delta_{\mathcal R}$ under once-per-epoch scheduling. The expectation is $\Delta_{\mathcal R}$ in all cases, but the intermittent schedules introduce greater correction variability than folded evaluation.

The relaxation normalisation rules out different step-size reduction with matched-pass count as the explanation. With the smooth objective, preconditioner, projection-work axis and relaxation history fixed, the observed differences arise from the component and subset organisation, including how the prior correction is scheduled. Total update counts still differ---within the folded schedule, for example, separate organisation performs 36 updates per matched pass and paired organisation 18---so the comparison is not compute-matched and establishes no wall-clock advantage.

\section{Prior-Aware Preconditioning}
\label{sec:optimisation_preconditioners}
\label{subsec:preconditioning}

Preconditioning can materially affect \gls{map} reconstruction because the data term and the edge-preserving prior can have very different local curvature scales \cite{clinthornePreconditioningMethodsImproved1993,ahnGloballyConvergentOrdered2002,depierroFastEMlikeMethods2001,ehrhardtFastPETReconstruction2025}. The comparison below includes data-only \gls{bsrem} scaling and four prior-aware curvature models for $\wdtnv$.

\subsection{Data Curvature}
\label{subsec:optimisation_data_preconditioning}

For modality $m$, let $s_m$ denote the sensitivity image on the shared grid,
\begin{equation}
    s_m
    =
    W_m A_m^\top \bm 1,
    \label{eq:shared_grid_sensitivity}
\end{equation}
where $A_m$ is the native-grid system matrix and $W_m$ maps native images to the shared prior grid. The data-only \gls{em}/\gls{bsrem} preconditioner is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{BSREM},m}
    =
    (z_m+\epsilon_x)\oslash s_m,
    \label{eq:bsrem_preconditioner}
\end{equation}
with element-wise division. This scaling models the data term only; it contains no prior curvature. Equivalently, the corresponding diagonal data Hessian surrogate is
\begin{equation}
    \widehat H_{\mathrm{data},m}
    =
    s_m \oslash (z_m+\epsilon_x).
    \label{eq:data_hessian_surrogate}
\end{equation}
The implementation optionally smooths the numerator image used in the \gls{em} preconditioner with a Gaussian filter. In the experiments below the smoothing \gls{fwhm} is $5\,\mathrm{mm}$ in each direction.

\subsection{Prior Curvature Models}
\label{subsec:optimisation_prior_preconditioning}

Four prior-curvature constructions were considered: three based on a local \acrfull{mm}/\acrfull{irls} surrogate and one based on the Lewis--Sendov Hessian of the smoothed spectral penalty.

\subsubsection{A quadratic majoriser in Jacobian space}
\label{subsubsec:jacobian_majoriser}

Let $Y,Y_0\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times M}$ with $d\geq M$, let $g$ be the Charbonnier function of Chapter~\ref{chapter:wdtnv}, let $\phi(Y)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}g(\sigma_\ell(Y))$ and write
\begin{equation}
    C_0 := \Omegaeps{Y_0} = \left(Y_0^\top Y_0 + \epsilon^2\mathbb{I}_M\right)^{-1/2}
    \label{eq:majoriser_weight}
\end{equation}
for the weight evaluated at the current point. Then, for all $Y$,
\begin{equation}
    \phi(Y)
    \;\leq\;
    \phi(Y_0)
    +\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}\!\left[\left(Y^\top Y - Y_0^\top Y_0\right)C_0\right],
    \label{eq:jacobian_majoriser}
\end{equation}
with equality at $Y=Y_0$.

The bound is the matrix form of the scalar construction in Figure~\ref{fig:majoriser_change_of_variable}. Although the Charbonnier potential $\psi(s)=\sqrt{s^2+\epsilon^2}-\epsilon$ is convex in $s$, it is concave in $s^2$. Its tangent in the variable $s^2$ is therefore an upper bound and becomes a quadratic in $s$. The matrix result follows by replacing $s^2$ with the Gram matrix $Y^\top Y$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{figures/preconditioning/majoriser_change_of_variable.png}
    \caption{Scalar form of the quadratic majoriser. In the variable $a=s^2$, the Charbonnier potential is concave and its tangent maps back to a quadratic upper bound in $s$. Here $\epsilon=0.3$ and $s_0=1.2$.}
    \label{fig:majoriser_change_of_variable}
\end{figure}

The eigenvalues of $Y^\top Y$ are $\sigma_\ell(Y)^2$ for $\ell=1,\dots,M$, so
\begin{equation}
    \phi(Y)
    =\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}\left(\sqrt{\sigma_\ell(Y)^2+\epsilon^2}-\epsilon\right)
    =\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\left(Y^\top Y+\epsilon^2\mathbb{I}_M\right)^{1/2}\right)-M\epsilon ,
    \label{eq:phi_as_trace}
\end{equation}
Writing $A=Y^\top Y$ therefore gives $\phi(Y)=f(A)$, where $f(A)=\operatorname{tr}((A+\epsilon^2\mathbb{I}_M)^{1/2})-M\epsilon$. The matrix square root is operator concave on the positive semidefinite cone \cite{bhatiaMatrixAnalysis1997a}, so $f$ is concave and obeys the tangent bound
\begin{equation}
    f(A)\;\leq\;f(A_0)+\operatorname{tr}\!\left[(A-A_0)\,\nabla f(A_0)\right],
    \qquad
    \nabla f(A_0)=\tfrac12 C_0 ,
    \label{eq:concave_tangent}
\end{equation}
and substituting $A=Y^\top Y$ and $A_0=Y_0^\top Y_0$ gives~\eqref{eq:jacobian_majoriser}. Since $C_0\succ0$, its right-hand side is a convex quadratic in $Y$ that touches $\phi$ at $Y_0$ and has gradient $Y_0C_0$ there, matching Equation~\eqref{eq:2x2_gram}. For $M=1$, $C_0=(s_0^2+\epsilon^2)^{-1/2}$ is the familiar half-quadratic or \gls{irls} weight for the Charbonnier potential \cite{fessler_image_2017, charbonnier_deterministic_1997}; Equation~\eqref{eq:majoriser_weight} is its spectral generalisation.

Summing~\eqref{eq:jacobian_majoriser} over voxels gives a frozen-anchor quadratic majoriser of the complete prior. Its Hessian has the form $\bm{D}^\ast(\cdot)\bm{D}$, where $\bm{D}$ collects the weighted directional finite-difference operators $\rho_{m,n}\Pi_{m,n}D$. This result is exact but spatially non-separable: neighbouring voxels remain coupled. The implemented models below reduce this curvature to diagonal or voxel-block form.

For the \gls{mm} family, let $k_0$ denote a preconditioner-refresh iteration and, at voxel $n$, define the anchored weighted directional Jacobian
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathcal{G}}_n^{(k_0)}
    :=
    \tilde{\mathcal{G}}_n\!\left(\bm z^{(k_0)}\right)
    =
    \left[
        \rho_{1,n}\Pi_{1,n}(Dz_1^{(k_0)})_n
        \;\middle|\;
        \rho_{2,n}\Pi_{2,n}(Dz_2^{(k_0)})_n
    \right]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times2}
    \label{eq:optimisation_weighted_jacobian}
\end{equation}
This is the weighted directional Jacobian from Chapter~\ref{chapter:wdtnv} (Equation~\eqref{eq:Yn_weighted}). At each preconditioner refresh, the modality-space \glsentryshort{mm} weight is
\begin{equation}
    C_n^{(k_0)}
    =
    \Omegaeps{\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_n^{(k_0)}}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        c_{11,n}^{(k_0)} & c_{12,n}^{(k_0)}\\
        c_{12,n}^{(k_0)} & c_{22,n}^{(k_0)}
    \end{pmatrix}.
    \label{eq:mm_weight_current_code}
\end{equation}
This is the spectral weight~\eqref{eq:spectral_weight}, evaluated at the refresh anchor and then held fixed until the next refresh. Below, the superscript is suppressed: $C_n$ and $c_{mm',n}$ denote these anchored quantities.

\subsubsection{Diagonal curvature models}

For diagonal preconditioning, the finite-difference stencil and directional projectors are collapsed to a per-voxel participation factor $\tau_{m,n}$. This factor includes the valid forward/backward stencil incidences, squared finite-difference scale factors and diagonal entries of $\Pi_{m,n}^\top\Pi_{m,n}$. The resulting local curvature approximation is
\begin{equation}
    \widehat H_{\mathcal R,m,n}^{\mathrm{loc\text{-}diag}}
    =
    V_{\mathrm{vox}}\,
    \rho_{m,n}^2\,c_{mm,n}\,\tau_{m,n}.
    \label{eq:diag_tight_preconditioner}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:diag_tight_preconditioner} is an implemented local approximation to the diagonal curvature; the compressed factor $\tau_{m,n}$ does not by itself identify it with the exact diagonal of a particular factorisation of the frozen quadratic. A more conservative Gershgorin-inflated model adds the magnitude of the cross-modal term,
\begin{equation}
    \widehat H_{\mathcal R,1,n}^{\mathrm{diag\text{-}G}}
    =
    V_{\mathrm{vox}}\,
    \rho_{1,n}^2\,(c_{11,n}+|c_{12,n}|)\,\tau_{1,n},
    \qquad
    \widehat H_{\mathcal R,2,n}^{\mathrm{diag\text{-}G}}
    =
    V_{\mathrm{vox}}\,
    \rho_{2,n}^2\,(c_{22,n}+|c_{12,n}|)\,\tau_{2,n}.
    \label{eq:diag_gersh_preconditioner}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Diagonal extraction versus a majorising diagonal}
\label{subsubsec:depierro_gap}

Simply extracting a diagonal from the non-separable Hessian does not preserve the bound~\eqref{eq:jacobian_majoriser}. De Pierro's convexity construction instead gives a separable upper bound \cite{depierroModifiedExpectationMaximization1995a}. For each row of a chosen factorisation $\mathcal{B}^\top\mathrm{diag}\{\breve c_i\}\mathcal{B}$, with $\breve c_i\geq0$, the direct row inequality is
\begin{equation*}
    \left(\sum_j b_{ij}v_j\right)^2
    \leq
    \left(\sum_j |b_{ij}|\right)
    \sum_j |b_{ij}|v_j^2.
\end{equation*}
Writing $b_i=\sum_j|b_{ij}|$ gives the factorisation-specific comparison
\begin{equation}
    d_j^{\mathrm{DP}}
    =
    \sum_i |b_{ij}|\,b_i\,\breve c_i ,
    \qquad\text{compared with}\qquad
    d_j^{\mathrm{diag}}
    =
    \sum_i b_{ij}^2\,\breve c_i .
    \label{eq:depierro_vs_diag}
\end{equation}
For this chosen factorisation, $d_j^{\mathrm{DP}}\geq d_j^{\mathrm{diag}}$. An unmixed first-order difference row with exactly the two entries $\pm1/\Delta_\ell$ contributes a factor of $2$ relative to its extracted diagonal. Once $\Pi_{m,n}$ mixes difference channels, the row support and coefficients depend on the stencil, projector and factorisation, so no universal factor is implied.

The Gershgorin construction~\eqref{eq:diag_gersh_preconditioner} inflates along the \emph{modality} index by adding $|c_{12,n}|$. It does not bound the spatial off-diagonal mass generated by the neighbour coupling in $\bm{D}^\ast(\cdot)\bm{D}$, and therefore does not close the spatial majorisation gap. This may explain why it tracks the local diagonal closely in Figure~\ref{fig:optim_preconditioners}.

An explicit De Pierro construction for a fixed factorisation would majorise the frozen quadratic prior surrogate, at the cost of a more conservative step. The implemented local diagonal~\eqref{eq:diag_tight_preconditioner}, however, uses the compressed $\tau_{m,n}$ approximation and is not automatically the $d_j^{\mathrm{diag}}$ of~\eqref{eq:depierro_vs_diag}. The latter equation therefore illustrates the spatial-diagonalisation gap rather than proving a bound for the implemented formula. No De Pierro model was implemented here.

\subsubsection{Voxel-block curvature models}

For block preconditioning, the \gls{pet} and \gls{spect} update components at each voxel are coupled by a $2\times2$ block. Let $\mathcal{N}(n)$ be the set of target voxels whose finite-difference stencil depends on source voxel $n$. For target voxel $r\in\mathcal{N}(n)$, let $c_{r\leftarrow n}\in\mathbb{R}^d$ be the finite-difference impulse vector produced in the directional channels when source voxel $n$ is perturbed. The local voxel-block curvature approximation uses
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_{r\leftarrow n}^{mm'}
    =
    c_{r\leftarrow n}^\top
    \Pi_{m,r}^\top
    \Pi_{m',r}
    c_{r\leftarrow n},
    \label{eq:block_alpha}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
    \widehat H_{\mathcal R,n}^{\mathrm{loc\text{-}block}}
    =
    V_{\mathrm{vox}}
    \sum_{r\in\mathcal{N}(n)}
    \left[
        B_r C_r B_r
    \right]
    \circ
    A_{r\leftarrow n},
    \qquad
    A_{r\leftarrow n}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \alpha_{r\leftarrow n}^{11} & \alpha_{r\leftarrow n}^{12}\\
        \alpha_{r\leftarrow n}^{21} & \alpha_{r\leftarrow n}^{22}
    \end{pmatrix},
    \label{eq:block_tight_preconditioner}
\end{equation}
where $B_r=\mathrm{diag}(\rho_{1,r},\rho_{2,r})$ and $\circ$ denotes the Hadamard product.

The fourth construction uses the exact Lewis--Sendov Hessian of the smoothed spectral penalty \cite{doi:10.1137/S089547980036838X}. If $H_{\mathcal R}=\nabla^2\mathcal R$ denotes the resulting image-space Hessian, the implemented approximation retains only its voxel-wise diagonal blocks,
\begin{equation}
    \widehat H_{\mathcal R}^{\mathrm{LS\text{-}block}}
    =
    \operatorname{blkdiag}
    \left(
        [H_{\mathcal R}]_{11},\ldots,[H_{\mathcal R}]_{NN}
    \right),
    \qquad
    [H_{\mathcal R}]_{nn}\in\mathbb R^{2\times2}.
    \label{eq:ls_block_preconditioner}
\end{equation}
It therefore retains \gls{pet}/\gls{spect} cross-curvature within each voxel but discards spatial off-diagonal blocks. Evaluating the smoothed spectral Hessian requires the voxel-wise singular-value decompositions used by the Lewis--Sendov construction. Recomputing this block model once per epoch consequently has a non-negligible cost.

This construction is Newton-like rather than \gls{mm}-like: it retains blocks of the exact local Hessian rather than reducing the quadratic majoriser above. Convexity gives $H_{\mathcal R}\succeq0$, but does not make these blocks invertible. Numerical flooring and, in the combined model, addition of positive data curvature make the practical inverse well defined and positive definite. The local Hessian of a non-quadratic function need not majorise that function, so this curvature model alone supplies no descent guarantee.

\subsubsection{Interaction with the non-negativity constraint}
\label{subsubsec:precond_constraint}

For a positive-definite preconditioner $\mathcal P$, the compatible projection onto the non-negative orthant is the metric projection
\begin{equation*}
    \Pi_{\mathbb{R}_+}^{\mathcal P^{-1}}(y)
    :=
    \underset{u\geq0}{\operatorname{argmin}}\;
    \tfrac12(u-y)^\top\mathcal P^{-1}(u-y).
\end{equation*}
When $\mathcal P$ is positive diagonal, this reduces to component-wise clipping. Off-diagonal \gls{pet}/\gls{spect} voxel blocks instead couple the constrained components, so the metric projection is generally not clipping. Consequently, the implemented block-preconditioned step followed by the Euclidean clipping in~\eqref{eq:optimisation_projected_update} is not the standard variable-metric projected-gradient step, and its usual guarantee does not transfer. This observation does not imply that the block method must fail; it identifies a gap between the implemented update and that convergence theory.

\subsection{Combining Data and Prior Curvature}
\label{subsec:optimisation_preconditioner_combination}

Let $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm D}=\widehat H_{\mathrm D}^{-1}$ denote the data preconditioner and $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal R}=\widehat H_{\mathcal R}^{-1}$ the preconditioner obtained from one of the prior-curvature models above. For block methods, $\widehat H_{\mathrm D}$ is the $2\times2$ diagonal matrix containing the modality-specific data curvatures at each voxel. The principal comparison combines the operands through the inverse-sum, or parallel-sum, rule
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{P}_{\parallel}
    =
    \left(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm D}^{-1}+\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal R}^{-1}\right)^{-1}
    =
    \left(\widehat H_{\mathrm D}+\widehat H_{\mathcal R}\right)^{-1}.
    \label{eq:parallel_sum_combination}
\end{equation}
The same expression applies to diagonal and voxel-block operands, with numerical floors used before inversion.

The \gls{mm} interpretation of~\eqref{eq:parallel_sum_combination} is conditional. If valid data and prior majorisers are constructed at a common anchor, their curvatures add, and the inverse of $\widehat H_{\mathrm D}+\widehat H_{\mathcal R}$ is the corresponding \gls{mm} preconditioner. Equation~\eqref{eq:parallel_sum_combination} always adds the selected curvature models, but a global \gls{mm} interpretation follows only when both operands satisfy that condition. None of the implemented diagonal or voxel-block reductions is established here as a global majoriser.

\subsubsection{Lehmer alternative}

The inverse sum belongs to a broader matrix Lehmer family. For voxel-wise symmetric positive-definite preconditioners $\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal R}$ and $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm D}$, define
\begin{equation}
    T=\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm D}^{-1/2}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal R}\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm D}^{-1/2},
    \qquad
    L_p(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal R},\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm D})
    =\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm D}^{1/2}f_p(T)\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm D}^{1/2},
    \qquad
    f_p(t)=\frac{t^p+1}{t^{p-1}+1},
    \label{eq:matrix_lehmer_combination}
\end{equation}
where $f_p(T)$ is evaluated spectrally. The implemented form is $\tfrac12L_p$; at $p=0$ it reproduces the inverse sum exactly,
\begin{equation}
    \tfrac12L_0(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal R},\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm D})
    =
    \left(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm D}^{-1}+\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal R}^{-1}\right)^{-1}.
    \label{eq:lehmer_parallel_equivalence}
\end{equation}
Thus $p=0$ has the conditional additive-majoriser interpretation above; other Lehmer orders are alternative combination rules without that guarantee.

\subsection{Experimental Protocol}
\label{subsec:preconditioner_experiment_methods}

The comparison uses the one-bed anthropomorphic phantom, modality-separated subsets, and the folded prior schedule, i.e.\ the complete prior gradient at every update. All methods use the same objective, initialisation, long-run reference, initial step $\varsigma^{(0)}=1$, and relaxation parameter $\eta_{\mathrm{relax}}=0.002$. Five stochastic realisations are evaluated for the first 20 matched data passes. Sensitivity-derived support masks are enabled with relative threshold $10^{-3}$, and the data component is smoothed with a $5\,\mathrm{mm}$ Gaussian \gls{fwhm}.

The preconditioner is updated once per epoch and held fixed between updates. This limits curvature-computation cost, particularly for the Lewis--Sendov construction, but means the preconditioner becomes progressively stale within each epoch.

The reference is separate from this five-method comparison: it is a single 1000-epoch run with the same objective, a Gershgorin-diagonal \gls{mm} preconditioner, and more conservative optimisation settings, including $\eta_{\mathrm{relax}}=10^{-4}$. It provides $x^\star$ for the \gls{nrmse} calculation rather than an additional candidate method.

\subsection{Interpretation and Limitations}

\subsubsection{Modality-dependent prior/data curvature balance}
\label{subsec:modality_curvature_balance}

A plausible explanation for the modality dependence in Figure~\ref{fig:optim_preconditioners} is the balance between the data and prior curvature. For the local diagonal model, a useful voxel-wise diagnostic is
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\widehat H_{\mathcal R,m,n}}{\widehat H_{\mathrm D,m,n}}
    =
    \frac{V_{\mathrm{vox}}\,\rho_{m,n}^{2}\,c_{mm,n}\,\tau_{m,n}\,(z_{m,n}+\epsilon_x)}{s_{m,n}} .
    \label{eq:curvature_ratio}
\end{equation}
The data curvature contains no explicit $\rho$ dependence, whereas the prior curvature contains $\rho_{m,n}^2$ and the \gls{mm} weight $c_{mm,n}$ formed from the weighted Jacobian. In a scalar or effectively uncoupled limit, this gives intuitive linear scaling with $\rho$ for large directional gradients and quadratic scaling near a flat region. For the coupled $2\times2$ problem, however, $c_{mm,n}$ depends on both modality columns and their cross-coupling, so these limits do not bound the net scaling. The ratio in~\eqref{eq:curvature_ratio} was not measured in the experiment and is offered only as local, diagonal intuition.

The weights $\rho_{m,n}$ were selected in Chapter~\ref{chapter:wdtnv} to balance gradients entering the nuclear norm, independently of the modality sensitivities. It is therefore plausible, but not established, that the different \gls{pet} and \gls{spect} trajectories reflect different prior/data curvature ratios. An untested extension would introduce a separate modality scaling for the preconditioner,
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda
    =
    \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{\mathrm{PET}},\lambda_{\mathrm{SPECT}}),
    \qquad
    \mathcal P_{\Lambda}
    =
    \left(
        \widehat H_{\mathrm D}
        +
        \Lambda^{1/2}\widehat H_{\mathcal R}\Lambda^{1/2}
    \right)^{-1},
    \qquad \lambda_{m}>0,
    \label{eq:modality_precond_weight}
\end{equation}
which reduces to modality-wise curvature scaling when the operands are diagonal. This changes the optimisation metric without changing the objective, unlike retuning $\rho_{m,n}$. The choice of $\Lambda$ was not investigated here.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:optimisation_conclusion}

Joint multi-bed reconstruction allowed the edge-preserving prior to act across the bed overlap and avoided the stitching artefacts visible in the qualitative example. In the one-bed subset experiment, every schedule used the same smooth objective and an unbiased uniform-sampling \gls{svrg} estimator. The relaxation rate was rescaled from the folded, modality-separated reference $\eta_{\mathrm{relax}}=0.002$, so every configuration had the same step size at a given matched pass. Under these controlled factors, the folded schedules reduced \gls{nrmse} over 20 passes whereas the intermittent schedules did not; the difference therefore reflects component and subset organisation. Total update counts differ, so this is not a wall-clock or compute-efficiency result.

In the preconditioner experiment, prior-aware scaling kept count-limited \gls{pet} bounded over the same 20-pass interval, while data-only \gls{bsrem} gave comparable or slightly lower \gls{spect} \gls{nrmse}. No prior-curvature construction was uniformly best. The local curvature ratio offers a physical interpretation of this modality dependence, but was not measured; the block-valid scaling in~\eqref{eq:modality_precond_weight} remains an untested extension.

The $p=0.01$ Lehmer result was only slightly below the parallel-sum \gls{nrmse} and also changes the effective step scale. Chapter~\ref{chapter:paper} retains the Lewis--Sendov block-diagonal/$p=0.01$.
