\chapter{Introduction to Weighted Directional Total Nuclear Variation}
\label{chapter:wdtnv}
In this chapter, we introduce a smoothed \acrfull{wdtnv} prior for synergistic emission tomography. The chapter focuses on the regulariser's relationship to total nuclear variation, its smooth Gram-matrix formulation, the use of anatomical information through directional operators, and the modality-wise weighting needed when coupling heterogeneous emission images.

The optimisation machinery used to apply this prior in large-scale \gls{pet}/\gls{spect} reconstruction, including prior-aware preconditioning and subset selection, is developed and tested on a single-bed phantom in Chapter~\ref{chapter:optimisation}. The extension to multi-bed \gls{pet} patient acquisitions is introduced in Chapter~\ref{chapter:paper}.

\section{A Gram-matrix formulation of Total Nuclear Variation}
\label{sec:tv_to_tnv}
We use the registered images $\bm{z}$ and voxel-wise Jacobian $\mathcal{G}_n(\bm{z})\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times M}$ introduced in Section~\ref{subsec:synergistic_recon}. For each voxel, define the Gram matrix
\begin{equation}
    G_n := \mathcal{G}_n(\bm{z})^\top\mathcal{G}_n(\bm{z})
    \in\mathbb{R}^{M\times M},
    \qquad G_n\succeq0.
    \label{eq:tnv_gram}
\end{equation}
Its eigenvalues are the squared singular values of the Jacobian: $\lambda_i(G_n)=\sigma_i(\mathcal{G}_n)^2$ for $i=1,\ldots,\min\{d,M\}$\footnote{If \(M>d\), the right Gram matrix \(G_n=\mathcal G_n^\top\mathcal G_n\in\mathbb R^{M\times M}\) has \(M-d\) additional zero eigenvalues compared with the smaller left Gram matrix \(\mathcal G_n\mathcal G_n^\top\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}\). Both have the same nonzero eigenvalues, so the penalty and its derivatives may instead be evaluated using the smaller matrix, with left singular vectors \(U\) replacing right singular vectors \(V\) in the corresponding formulas. Matrix dimensions must be adjusted accordingly; in the smoothed trace formulation below, the subtraction \(M\epsilon\) becomes \(d\epsilon\), so additional zero eigenvalues contribute zero.}. TNV from~\eqref{eq:tnv} then has the equivalent representation
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{TNV}}(\bm{z})
    =\sum_{n=1}^N\|\mathcal{G}_n(\bm{z})\|_*
    =\sum_{n=1}^N\sum_{i=1}^{M}\sqrt{\lambda_i(G_n)}.
    \label{eq:tnv_gram_spectral}
\end{equation}
The singular-value and Gram-eigenvalue expressions describe the same penalty. The former explains the low-rank coupling discussed in Chapter~\ref{chapter:reconstruction}; the latter provides a convenient form for evaluating the penalty and its derivatives. For two emission modalities, the Gram matrix is only $2\times2$, regardless of the number of finite-difference directions $d$.

\subsection{Mathematical properties and differentiability}
\label{subsec:tnv_differentiability}

Like \gls{tv}, TNV is not differentiable everywhere; in particular, the nuclear norm is non-differentiable at rank-deficient Jacobians. We use the Charbonnier smoothing discussed for \gls{tv} in Chapter~\ref{chapter:reconstruction}, applied to each singular value \cite{charbonnier_deterministic_1997,fessler_image_2017}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:charbonnier_g}
g(s)=\sqrt{s^2+\epsilon^2}-\epsilon,
\qquad
g'(s)=\frac{s}{\sqrt{s^2+\epsilon^2}},
\qquad
g''(s)=\frac{\epsilon^2}{(s^2+\epsilon^2)^{3/2}},
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon>0$ controls the degree of smoothing.

For the following local calculations, we suppress the voxel index $n$ and the dependence on $\bm{z}$, writing $\mathcal{G}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times M}$ for the local Jacobian and $G=\mathcal{G}^\top\mathcal{G}$. The resulting formulas also apply to the weighted directional Jacobian introduced below. Using the equivalence above, the smoothed local penalty is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \phi(\mathcal{G})
        &:=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\left(\sqrt{\lambda_i(G)+\epsilon^2}-\epsilon\right)\\
        &=\operatorname{tr}\!\left((G+\epsilon^2\mathbb{I}_M)^{1/2}\right)-M\epsilon.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:phi_gram_spectral}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $\phi(\mathcal{G})=\sum_{i=1}^{R}g(\sigma_i(\mathcal{G}))$ with $R=\min\{d,M\}$ as in~\eqref{eq:schatten}; any additional zero Gram eigenvalues contribute zero because $g(0)=0$. This representation also connects the prior to the calculus of spectral functions of symmetric matrices used for its Hessian in Chapter~\ref{chapter:optimisation}.

\paragraph{Convexity.}
The singular-value representation gives a direct convexity argument. Define $f_\epsilon(s)=\sum_{i=1}^{R}g(s_i)$ on $\mathbb{R}^R$, so that $\phi=f_\epsilon\circ\sigma$. Since $g$ is even and convex, $f_\epsilon$ is convex and absolutely symmetric, meaning invariant under permutations and sign changes of its arguments. Hence $\phi$ is convex on $\mathbb{R}^{d\times M}$ \cite[Corollary~2.6]{lewisConvexAnalysisUnitarily1995}. Smoothness of $f_\epsilon$ also ensures that $\phi$ is differentiable even when $\mathcal{G}$ is rank deficient \cite[Theorem~3.1]{lewisConvexAnalysisUnitarily1995}. The finite-difference operator $D$, directional operators $\Pi_{m,n}$ of Section~\ref{subsec:directional_from_guidance}, and weight matrices $B_n$ of Section~\ref{subsec:modality_weighting} are fixed linear maps. Composing $\phi$ with these maps and summing over voxels preserves convexity, so
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathrm{w\text{-}dTNV}} \ \text{is convex on}\ \mathbb{R}^{N\times M}.
    \label{eq:wdtnv_convex}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Jacobian-space gradient.}
Write $\phi(\mathcal{G})=h(G)$ as in~\eqref{eq:phi_gram_spectral}, with $G=V\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i)V^\top$ and $V\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times M}$ orthogonal. Lewis's spectral gradient formula \cite[Theorem~1.1]{lewisDerivativesSpectralFunctions1996} gives
\begin{equation}
    \nabla h(G)
    =\frac12 V\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_i+\epsilon^2}}\right)V^\top
    =\frac12(G+\epsilon^2\mathbb{I}_M)^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
    \Omegaeps{\mathcal{G}} \coloneqq \left(\mathcal{G}^\top \mathcal{G}+\epsilon^2\mathbb{I}\right)^{-1/2}
    \in\mathbb{R}^{M\times M}.
    \label{eq:spectral_weight}
\end{equation}
Because $\epsilon>0$, $\Omegaeps{\mathcal{G}}$ is well defined and \gls{spd} for every $\mathcal{G}$, including rank-deficient ones. The chain rule through $G=\mathcal{G}^\top\mathcal{G}$ then gives the Jacobian-space gradient
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi_grad_inv_sqrt}
\nabla\phi(\mathcal{G})=2\mathcal{G}\,\nabla h(G)=\mathcal{G}\,\Omegaeps{\mathcal{G}},
\end{equation}
obtained by applying the inverse square-root of an $M\times M$ \gls{spd} matrix to the columns of $\mathcal{G}$. For $M=2$ this inverse square-root has a closed form (Appendix~\ref{app:inv_sqrt_2x2}), so the gradient can be evaluated using only dot products and scalar square-roots per voxel, without an eigendecomposition\footnote{For the case where $M>d$, $d=3$, $G=\mathcal{G}\mathcal{G}^\top$ one can use Kopp's cubic eigenvalue decomposition and fallback algorithms defined in \cite{koppEfficientNumericalDiagonalization2008}.}.

\section{Weighted Directional Total Nuclear Variation ($\wdtnv$)}
\label{sec:wdtnv}

This section extends vanilla TNV \cite{rigieJointReconstructionMultichannel2015, holtTotalNuclearVariation2014} in two ways:
\begin{itemize}
    \item anatomical information is incorporated via a directional operator (similar to \gls{dtv}, Equation~\eqref{eq:dtv_defn})
    \item  voxel- and modality-specific diagonal weighting can account for differences in scale and allow each modality to be down-weighted in regions where its measurements are less reliable.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Directional operators from guidance}
\label{subsec:directional_from_guidance}

A straightforward way to extend (smoothed) \gls{tv} and TNV is by appending the guidance image as an additional modality. However, this has several disadvantages. A single global scaling would have data-dependent and spatially non-uniform consequences. Where emission gradients are weak, even modest guidance gradients could dominate the local Jacobian and hence the singular values; where emission gradients are strong, the same scaling may become negligible.

We instead use the directional operator used in \gls{dtv} (Equation~\ref{eq:directional_op}) to incorporate a higher-resolution anatomical image. The image steers the penalty rather than entering the joint Jacobian as an additional column. This keeps the anatomical channel separate from the emission channels, whose units, resolution, and artefacts differ (as demonstrated in Chapter~\ref{chapter:deconvolution_pvc}).

Each modality gradient $(D z_m)_n$ is replaced by its directional counterpart $\Pi_{m,n}(D z_m)_n$.  $\Pi_{m,n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ is constructed from the guidance field at voxel $n$
\begin{equation}
\xi_n
=
\frac{(D u)_n}{\sqrt{\|(D u)_n\|_2^2+\eta^2}},
\qquad
\Pi_{m,n}=\mathbb{I}_d-\gamma_m\,\xi_n\xi_n^\top,
\qquad \gamma_m\in[0,1].
\label{eq:wdtnv_directional_operator}
\end{equation}
In homogeneous regions of the guidance image, $\xi_n$ is small and $\Pi_{m,n}$ is close to the identity. 

Using \gls{ct} to construct the directional operator  decouples anatomical steering from emission-to-emission weighting. Modality weights determine how strongly emission gradients are coupled to one another through the nuclear norm, while $\Pi_{m,n}$ determines how strongly the penalty prefers guidance-consistent edge directions. Retaining a two-modality Jacobian also preserves the efficient $2\times2$ Gram-matrix structure derived in Section~\ref{sec:tv_to_tnv}.

\subsection{Modality-wise weighting and the full \texorpdfstring{$\wdtnv$}{w-dTNV} prior}
\label{subsec:modality_weighting}

\gls{pet} and \gls{spect} images of the same administered \gls{y90} activity differ substantially in dynamic range: the effective count level, sensitivity, and noise characteristics vary by orders of magnitude between the two modalities. If the directional emission gradients $\Pi_{m,n}(D z_m)_n$ are used directly without normalisation, the nuclear-norm coupling is dominated by the modality with the largest dynamic range.

To compensate, we introduce voxel-wise, modality-wise diagonal weight matrices
\begin{equation}
    B_n := \mathrm{diag}(\rho_{1,n},\,\rho_{2,n}) \in \mathbb{R}^{2\times 2},
    \qquad \rho_{m,n} > 0.
    \label{eq:Bn_def}
\end{equation}
The entries $\rho_{m,n}$ are the weights applied to the modality gradients and may incorporate both intensity normalisation and regularisation settings. They are distinct from a global scalar $\beta$ multiplying the entire prior and from the user-controlled modality settings $\beta_m$. The application in Chapter~\ref{chapter:paper} uses spatially constant entries $\rho_{m,n}=\rho_m$, obtained by dividing $\beta_m$ by the estimated modality dynamic range.
The weighted directional Jacobian is then
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathcal{G}}_n(\bm{z}) := \bigl[\Pi_{1,n}(D z_1)_n \;\big|\; \Pi_{2,n}(D z_2)_n\bigr]\,B_n
    \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times 2}.
    \label{eq:Yn_weighted}
\end{equation}

Throughout, $B_n$ is treated as a fixed operator that does not depend on $\bm{z}$. In the application of Chapter~\ref{chapter:paper} the weights are computed once, from early-stopped unregularised reconstructions, and then held fixed.

The weighted directional Total Nuclear Variation ($\wdtnv$) prior is then
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathrm{w\text{-}dTNV}}(\bm{z})
    :=
    V_{\mathrm{vox}}\sum_{n=1}^{N} \phi\!\left(\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_n(\bm{z})\right),
    \label{eq:wdtnv_prior}
\end{equation}
where $V_{\mathrm{vox}}$ is the voxel volume and $\phi$ is the smoothed Gram-matrix potential from~\eqref{eq:phi_gram_spectral}. Choosing equal weights $\rho_{m,n}\equiv 1$ recovers unweighted $d$TNV. Choosing $\rho_{m,n}$ proportional to the inverse of a characteristic modality scale brings both channels to a comparable range before the nuclear-norm coupling is evaluated. 

\paragraph{Image-space gradient.}
By~\eqref{eq:phi_grad_inv_sqrt} and the chain rule through the linear map $\bm{z}\mapsto\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_n(\bm{z})$, the gradient of the prior with respect to each modality image is
\begin{equation}
    \nabla_{z_m}\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathrm{w\text{-}dTNV}}(\bm{z})
    =
    V_{\mathrm{vox}}\,D^\ast\!\left(\rho_{m,n}\,\Pi_{m,n}^\top
    \bigl[\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_n\,\Omegaeps{\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_n}\bigr]_{:,m}\right)_{n=1}^{N},
    \qquad m=1,2,
    \label{eq:wdtnv_gradient}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_n=\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_n(\bm{z})$, $[\cdot]_{:,m}$ denotes the $m$-th column, and the adjoint $D^\ast$ acts on the field of $d$-vectors indexed by $n$.

\section{Conclusion}

This chapter introduced a smoothed, weighted, and directional Total Nuclear Variation ($\wdtnv$) prior for synergistic reconstruction. Charbonnier smoothing of the singular values results in a differentiable and convex spectral functional whose voxel-wise Jacobian-space gradient can be written in Gram-matrix form. Convexity is inherited from smoothed TNV because $D$, $\Pi_{m,n}$ and $B_n$ are all fixed linear maps. For two coupled emission modalities the key spectral operation reduces to the analytical calculation of the inverse square-root of   $2\times2$ \gls{spd} matrices, allowing the prior and its gradient to be evaluated robustly without a general per-voxel \glsentryshort{svd}.

The chapter also separated two roles that are easily confounded in multimodality reconstruction. Anatomical information is used directionally through $\Pi_{m,n}$, steering the penalty without adding \gls{ct} as another coupled intensity channel. Modality scaling is handled separately through the diagonal weight matrix $B_n$, allowing \gls{pet} and \gls{spect} gradients to enter the nuclear-norm coupling on comparable scales. Chapter~\ref{chapter:optimisation} uses these definitions to construct practical preconditioners and subset-selection strategies for large-scale $\wdtnv$ optimisation.
