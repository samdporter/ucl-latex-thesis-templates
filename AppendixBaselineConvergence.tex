\chapter{Long-Run Reference Diagnostics}
\label{app:baseline_convergence}

The single reference run in Chapter~\ref{chapter:optimisation} used the same objective as the short-run comparisons: 1000 epochs, 36 stochastic updates per epoch, a Gershgorin-diagonal \glsentryshort{mm} preconditioner and $\eta_{\mathrm{relax}}=10^{-4}$. Here $\mathcal O_k=\mathcal O(\bm z^{(k)})$ and $\mathcal O^\star=\min_{0\leq k\leq1000}\mathcal O_k$ denote the recorded and best observed objectives.

Table~\ref{tab:app_baseline_stationarity} summarises the convergence diagnostics;
relative \glsentryshort{pet} iterate velocity is
$\lVert x^{(k)}-x^{(k-1)}\rVert/\lVert x^{(k)}\rVert$.

\begin{table}[htbp]
    \centering
    \caption{Final diagnostics for the reference reconstruction.}
    \label{tab:app_baseline_stationarity}
    \small
    \begin{tabular}{p{0.68\linewidth}r}
        \toprule
        Quantity & Value\\
        \midrule
        Final objective gap / observed objective decrease & $8.819\times10^{-9}$\\
        Final relative objective change per epoch & $1.717\times10^{-11}$\\
        Final PET iterate velocity per epoch & $7.285\times10^{-8}$\\
        Final relaxed step size & $0.2174$\\
        Epoch reaching 99.9\% of observed decrease & $45$\\
        Step size at that epoch & $0.8606$\\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The small objective change and iterate velocity support effective stationarity; the appreciable final step size argues against step-size starvation. This is a numerical reference, not ground truth or a proven global minimiser. Figure~\ref{fig:optim_reference_pair} shows the final images and evaluation regions.
